Order to Output

Fourteen days of factory data in six CSV files. Build a database, an API and a frontend on top of it, and deploy it.

The problem

A factory has four machines that assemble ceiling fans. Each machine works on one order at a time.

An order says which product to make, how many, by when, and on which machine. Every unit a machine finishes is recorded. Every time a machine stops — maintenance, no material, a fault — that is recorded too, with a reason.

You are given 14 days of that history as CSV files. Three groups of people need to work with it.

The people

Two people · at a desk · laptops

The planning team

They own the order book. Sales sends them new orders through the day, which they add to the system and assign to a machine. When priorities change, a customer pulls an order forward, or material runs out, they are the ones who update it.

Most of their day is spent answering questions: what is the status of this order, what is due this week, what has not been started yet. The order book holds 68 orders today. It will hold thousands.

One person · on the floor · a 1920 × 1080 display on the wall

The floor supervisor

Their job is keeping the four machines running. They spend the shift walking between them, usually with their hands full, and they do not carry a laptop.

The display is mounted high on the wall above the machines. They look up at it in passing, from several metres away, for a second or two at a time. What they are looking for is whether anything needs them right now.

Nobody operates this display. There is no mouse and no keyboard, and it has to stay correct without anyone touching it.

One person · in meetings · a tablet

The plant manager

They are responsible for how the factory performs over weeks, not minutes. They want to understand a period they choose: whether output is where it should be, whether things are getting better or worse, and what is costing the factory the most.

What they conclude turns into decisions about staffing, when to schedule maintenance, and which problem to fix first.

The data

machines.csv The machines on the floor, and the output rate each is expected to sustain. 4
skus.csv The products the factory makes, and the standard time to build one unit of each. 8
orders.csv What to make, how many, by when, on which machine, and where each order has got to. 68
unit_events.csv One row for every unit a machine finished. 80,533
downtime_events.csv One row for every time a machine stopped, with when it started, when it ended and why. 307
downtime_reasons.csv What each stoppage reason means, and whether it was planned or unplanned. 10

Treat 2026-08-17 09:15 as the current time. The data is a saved snapshot, not a live feed.

Timestamps in the files are plain local factory time. No timezones.

01

Database

Design a relational schema for this data and write a script that loads the CSVs into it.

Write the CREATE TABLE statements yourself rather than generating them from an ORM.

02

API

A REST API in Node or Python.

  • The dataset is 80,000 rows and will grow.
  • Requests sent directly to the API, bypassing your UI, must be validated server-side.
03

Frontend

Build the frontend in React, consuming your own API, so that each of the three people above can do their job.

You are building for three different kinds of user, and they should not all be given the same screen:

  • Management want a summarised view of a period they choose — enough to see how the factory is doing and decide what to act on. Depth matters more than immediacy here.
  • Factory floor staff want the immediate picture and nothing else. They are working, not browsing: it has to be readable at a glance, from a distance, with no interaction at all.
  • Desk staff want detail and control. They search, create and change things, so this is where forms, validation and clear feedback when something is rejected actually matter.
04

Deployment

Deploy the application and give us a link we can open. Where you host it is your choice.
